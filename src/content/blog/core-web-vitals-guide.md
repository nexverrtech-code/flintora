A slow website is usually filed under technical problems. It belongs under marketing problems. Every extra second of load time loses visitors you already paid to attract.

## The three numbers that matter

Google's Core Web Vitals measure how a page feels to a real person.

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)** measures how quickly the main content appears. Aim for 2.5 seconds or less.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP)** measures how quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicks. Aim for 200 milliseconds or less.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)** measures how much the page jumps around while loading. Aim for 0.1 or less.

## Why marketers should care

Speed affects three things you are measured on.

1. **Rankings.** Page experience is part of how Google evaluates pages.
2. **Ad costs.** Landing page experience feeds into Quality Score, which affects what you pay per click.
3. **Conversion.** People leave slow pages. Faster pages keep more of the traffic you worked for.

## Fixes that usually pay off first

You rarely need a rebuild. Start here.

- **Compress and resize images.** Serve modern formats such as WebP or AVIF at the size they are displayed.
- **Load the important things first.** Preload the hero image and fonts. Defer everything else.
- **Cut unused scripts.** Old tracking tags, chat widgets and plugins add up quickly.
- **Reserve space.** Give images, embeds and ads fixed dimensions so the layout does not shift.
- **Use a content delivery network.** Serve files from a location close to the visitor.

## How to check your own site

Run your key pages through PageSpeed Insights and look at the field data, which reflects real visitors. Then check the Core Web Vitals report in Google Search Console to see which page groups need attention.

Fix the templates that carry the most traffic first. A faster product or service page template improves every page that uses it.
