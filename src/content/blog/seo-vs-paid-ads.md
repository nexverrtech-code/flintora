Most growing businesses ask the same question early on: should we invest in SEO or in paid ads? The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right split depends on your timeline.

## What paid ads do well

Paid search and paid social buy you speed. You can be in front of buyers this afternoon, test an offer this week and know within a month whether the numbers work.

- **Fast feedback.** You learn which messages and audiences respond.
- **Control.** You decide the budget, the audience and the landing page.
- **Predictability.** Once a campaign works, more budget usually brings more results, up to a point.

The trade off is simple. When the budget stops, the traffic stops.

## What SEO does well

SEO builds an asset. A page that ranks keeps bringing visitors without a cost per click, and a site with strong authority finds every new page easier to rank.

- **Compounding returns.** Work done this quarter keeps paying next year.
- **Trust.** Many buyers skip the ads and click the organic results.
- **Lower long term cost.** Cost per acquisition tends to fall as rankings mature.

The trade off here is time. Competitive terms usually need three to six months of steady work.

## A simple way to decide

Ask three questions.

1. **How soon do you need revenue?** If the answer is this quarter, lead with paid.
2. **Is there existing search demand?** If people already search for what you sell, SEO has a clear path. If you are creating a category, paid social and content come first.
3. **How long will you compete here?** If this is a long game, start SEO now, because the clock only starts when you do.

## A sensible starting split

For many businesses, a reasonable first year looks like this:

- **Months one to three:** most of the budget in paid, to validate the offer and learn the language buyers respond to. Fix technical SEO in the background.
- **Months four to nine:** shift toward content and SEO, using the keywords and messages that paid has already proven.
- **Month ten onward:** organic carries more of the load, and paid focuses on launches, remarketing and the highest intent terms.

## Use each to make the other better

The best results come when the channels share what they learn. Search term reports show which keywords convert, so SEO knows what to target. Organic pages that rank can take pressure off paid budgets. Strong landing pages improve both.

Treat SEO and paid as one system with two speeds, and the budget question gets much easier to answer.
